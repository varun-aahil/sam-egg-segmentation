1. Executive Summary

This report presents an end-to-end computer vision solution for egg segmentation and multi-object tracking under challenging conditions, including touching instances, boiling water, high-contrast table textures, and hand occlusions. Ground truth masks were established via SAM (vit_b on CUDA) with human point/box prompts and an automated area and border contact audit. A classical image-processing pipeline was tuned strictly on the dev split across 18 controlled trials and evaluated once on the unseen eval split. A multi-object tracker was deployed on the 1440-frame table experiment video, incorporating YCrCb skin gating to eliminate hand occlusion leakage. All metrics, counts, and tables presented below are derived directly from runs of this repository.

  • Ground Truth Integrity: 110 clean SAM masks generated via SamPredictor (vit_b on CUDA) using human point prompts and tight bounding boxes for boiling water edge cases. 0 flagged suspicious masks remaining.
  • Classical Pipeline (Dev Split, n=88): Pixel Mean IoU = 0.4018 (95% CI: [0.3435, 0.4603], Median = 0.3147), Dice = 0.5200, Detection F1 = 0.4087, Mean Count Error = 2.20.
  • Classical Pipeline (Eval Split, n=22): Pixel Mean IoU = 0.3196 (95% CI: [0.1978, 0.4268], Median = 0.2502), Dice = 0.4239, Detection F1 = 0.3206, Mean Count Error = 1.68.
  • Video Tracking (1440 frames): 1440 frames processed at 24.0 FPS. 6 unique tracks created across the video, with 18 loss events and 12 recoveries. Hand occlusion and fingertip leakage at frame 600 resolved using YCrCb skin gating and morphological opening (gating reduced the total foreground mask by 2296 px over the whole frame; the fingertip bulge is visible in Figure 6, and restricted to the crop x in [200, 460], y in [300, 430], the mask was reduced by 1304 px).

2. Task 1: Ground Truth Mask Generation (SAM)

SAM (vit_b, 375 MB checkpoint) was executed on CUDA using saved point prompts from points.json with multi-mask score selection (multimask_output=True). Hand-tuned shape and color filters were strictly excluded from the ground-truth path to prevent artificial bias toward the classical segmentation pipeline.

Ground Truth Egg Distribution across 110 images: 93 images (84.5%) with 1 egg; 6 images (5.5%) with 2 eggs; 11 images (10.0%) with 3 eggs.

Ground Truth Quality Audit & Fixes (out/sam/gt_fix_audit.json)

Initial automated SAM passes on boiling water images flooded the pot interior. An automated border and area audit in src/contact_sheet.py flagged masks with area > 40% or edge contact > 50%. Bounding box prompts were added around egg boundaries for boiling pot scenes (verified in out/sam/gt_fix_audit.json):

  • not_damaged_124: BBox [280, 150, 520, 400] reduced area fraction from 81.9% (335354 px, 978 border contacts) to 13.4% (54995 px, 0 border contacts).
  • not_damaged_135: BBox [270, 170, 520, 420] reduced area fraction from 79.5% (325584 px, 994 border contacts) to 14.2% (58113 px, 0 border contacts).
  • not_damaged_145: BBox [250, 200, 480, 450] reduced area fraction from 62.5% (256010 px, 665 border contacts) to 13.2% (54032 px, 0 border contacts).

All 110 ground truth masks now pass the automated audit (0 flagged in out/sam/suspicious.txt).

3. Task 2: Parameterized Classical Segmentation & Experiment Log

A unified parameterized pipeline was tuned on the 88 dev images across 18 controlled trials. Every trial varied exactly one parameter relative to its named parent. Keep/discard decisions compare strictly against the parent's dev IoU. Note: Trial watershed_conditional modified two parameters relative to its parent (area_filter_2500): it enabled conditional watershed and set the peak distance threshold to min_dist=50. Under this trial, Detection F1 rose from 0.3084 to 0.4087, mean signed count error rose from +0.45 to +2.20 (over-splitting), pixel IoU unchanged. Because Detection F1 improved substantially for instance segmentation, it was kept for the final configuration.

Bootstrap 95% Confidence Intervals & Dev vs Eval Analysis

Inspection of per-category results reveals the concrete factors driving the gap between dev (0.4018) and eval (0.3196):

  1. Severe Hand Image Degradation: Hand-held egg images average IoU 0.6120 in dev (11 images) but collapse to 0.1502 in eval (5 images). Hand shots represent 22.7% of the eval split vs 12.5% of dev.
  2. Tabletop Contrast Differences: Tabletop scenes average IoU 0.3659 in dev (32 images) vs 0.2424 in eval (8 images).
  3. Absence of White-Tray Camouflage in Eval: All 13 white-tray images (IoU 0.1274) were grouped into dev, while eval contains 0.
  4. Small-Sample Variance: Because eval has only 22 images, its bootstrap 95% confidence interval spans widely ([0.1978, 0.4268]) and encompasses the dev mean. Thus, while hand-holding failures and tabletop lighting differences clearly drive the performance drop in the observed data, small sample variance also plays a substantial role.

5. Task 4: Multi-Object Video Tracking

Tracking was evaluated on the 1440-frame table sequence (1280x720 @ 24.0 fps). Eggs are placed on the table at frame 222, spun, occluded by hand, and removed before frame 1005. The tracker maintains persistent IDs using Hungarian matching on IoU + distance with constant-velocity prediction.

  • Fingertip Leakage Resolution: At frame 600, demonstrator hand contact caused baseline thresholding to leak into fingers. Incorporating YCrCb skin-color gating (Cr ∈ [135, 175], Cb ∈ [80, 125]) and larger opening eliminated false skin contour (gating reduced the total foreground mask by 2296 px over the whole frame; the fingertip bulge is visible in Figure 6, and restricted to the crop x in [200, 460], y in [300, 430], the mask was reduced by 1304 px).
  • Track Lifetimes & Event Statistics: 6 unique tracks were created, with 18 loss events and 12 recoveries. Lifetimes: Egg 1 (529 frames), Egg 2 (282 frames), Egg 3 (101 frames), Egg 4 (269 frames), Egg 5 (3 frames), Egg 6 (190 frames).

Analysis of Track 5 from Event Stills

Track 5 was created at frame 800 and was matched at frames 800, 801, and 810 (3 matched frames total, area ~3,043 to 3,782 px around centroid x=721, y=531). Inspection of the saved stills (frames 798, 800, 802, 806, 810, 814) reveals its exact origin:

  1. Motion-Blur Fragmentation: When the raw egg is spun at high angular velocity, severe rotational motion blur and specular reflection split the egg segmentation into two distinct fragments.
  2. Coexistence with Track 6 at Frame 810: At frame 810, the left fragment was associated with Track 5 (which was within the 150 px re-ID radius), while the newly appearing right fragment at x=786 spawned Track 6.
  3. Consolidation: By frame 814, the spinning egg consolidated into a single contour at x=777 (area 21,297 px), which was tracked continuously by Track 6 until table clearance (frame 999). Track 5 received no further detections and was purged.
  4. Production Recommendation: Spurious transient tracks with lifetimes under 5 frames should be filtered as false positives.

6. Limitations and Future Work

  1. Ground Truth Masks: Ground truth masks were generated using SAM (vit_b) with human point/box prompts rather than dense manual polygon tracings.
  2. Dataset Size: With 88 dev and 22 eval images, sample sizes are limited, leading to wide bootstrap confidence intervals on eval.
  3. Watershed Behavior: Under trial watershed_conditional, Detection F1 rose from 0.3084 to 0.4087, mean signed count error rose from +0.45 to +2.20 (over-splitting), pixel IoU unchanged. Distance transform watershed partitions instances without changing overall outer foreground boundaries.
  4. Domain-Specific Tuning: Video tracking parameters (table ROI y ≥ 270, watermark bounds, YCrCb skin thresholds) were calibrated for this specific camera viewpoint and table illumination. Generalizing to varied environments would require automated surface or person detection.
  5. Re-Identification Across Removals: Because white eggs lack distinctive surface texture, tracking identities across complete table removals and replacements is physically ambiguous under classical tracking.
  6. Future Extensions: Fine-tuning a deep semantic segmentation model (e.g. YOLOv8-seg or Mask2Former) on the dev set using SAM pseudo-masks could learn contextual semantic priors (such as distinguishing eggs from white trays). Combining this with dense optical flow could bridge severe hand occlusions during spinning.
